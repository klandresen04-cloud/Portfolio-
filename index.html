Featured Research

Data Centers VS Housing Prices

Economics Research Project

Examining the relationship between data centers and nearby housing values

Project Overview

Data centers provide essential digital infrastructure, but their development may also create costs for nearby communities. These possible effects include noise, visual disruption, increased energy demand, construction activity, and pressure on local infrastructure.

This research explored whether the manufacturing of data centers may be associated with changes in nearby residential property values.

Research Question

Does the manufacturing of data centers cause negative externalities to surrounding housing prices?

Research Process

01
Defined the research question

Multiple protest's were being held during the start up of the Monterey Park Data Center. Residents were concerned about the potential increase in noise, electrical demand, and property value.

02
Collected and reviewed data

Obtained housing pice data from Redfin, focusing on sqft, bed, and bath.

03
Prepared the dataset

Organized, cleaned, and compared data through an Excel document.

04
Analyzed the relationship

Applied regression in software R, to see a change in Virginia housing values before and after data centers were built. Then used the change in percentage using Stata for a difference-in-difference framework to see statistical change within Monterey Park housing prices.

05
Communicated the findings

Presented the research question, theories, results, limitations, and conclusions through a research paper and presentation.

Economic Concepts

Negative Externalities

Costs from economic activity that may affect people who are not directly involved in the transaction.

Hedonic Pricing

Valuation method that estimates how specific internal features and external environmental factors affect the overall market price of a good or service.

Regression Model

Statistical tool used to estimate the relationship between a dependent target variable and one or more independent predictor variables.

Difference-in-Differences

Statistical method used to estimate the causal effect of a specific intervention or policy.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my ability to take a controversial topic and turn it into a structured economic research process.

I gained experience working with data, examining possible relationships between variables, considering alternative explanations, and communicating conclusions without overstating what the evidence could prove.

Research Findings

The analysis found that there was no statistically signifigant evidence that data centers affecting nearby housing prices. However the results were limited by the timeframe, and the small set of varibales that I could obtain. Additional research and data could examine a broader idea
